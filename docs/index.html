Visual identity

Humanity is entering an age when intelligence is no longer exclusively human.

AI, automation and robotics are becoming part of how we learn, work, create and decide. Yet technological progress does not automatically become human progress. Without new forms of cooperation, it can concentrate power, deepen inequality, and leave people behind.

Robo Co-op begins with a different premise: the future is not Human versus Machine, but Human with Machine.

ROBO
intelligence, technology, and machines.
CO-OP
cooperation, solidarity, and shared agency.

Their intersection creates an infinite loop in which people and machines continuously learn, build and improve together — expanding capabilities and possibilities that neither could achieve alone.

We do not automate humanity away. We update humanity through solidarity.

The mark system

One motif, four states — from Infinity to Plurality.

ROBO represents intelligence, technology and machines. CO-OP represents cooperation, solidarity and shared agency. Their intersection creates an infinite loop in which people and machines continuously learn, build and improve together. Infinity is what two create together. Plurality is what many create together.

Infinity

Machine × Humanity

The two lobes are Machine and Humanity, locked into one loop. Learning is never solo: two or more, learning cooperatively — the group has always been our unit of education.

Robo Co-op is the composite mark: not one loop but many, overlapping. Plurality is the order above infinity — many minds, many machines, and many ways of owning what they build together.

Logos

Three lockups per brand, each in up to four finishes. Every file is vector; PNGs sit alongside in assets/logos/<brand>/png/ for contexts that cannot take SVG.

Only Robo Co-op has Black and White versions Its mark is black, so it disappears on a dark surface and needs white artwork. Robo Lab, Robo University and Co-op Lab are legible on both and use a single brand-coloured file. Every download below keeps its real colours.

Logo rules

Never do this to the logo

  • ✕Rotate or distort the logo
  • ✕Use a colour other than the brand’s own
  • ✕Typeset the wordmark in a different font
  • ✕Disassemble the logo
  • ✕Reposition or resize the elements of the logo
  • ✕Pair the logo with a mark other than the official one
  • ✕Use low-resolution versions of the logo
  • ✕Place the logo over an image that makes it illegible
  • ✕Change or alter the text
  • ✕Use an opacity setting below 50%

Co-branding

  • ✕Make the partner logo appear more dominant
  • ✕Overlap the logos
  • ✕Stagger the logos
  • ✕Change the partner logo’s colours to match ours

Correct co-branding

Partner logo

Equal optical weight, a fixed divider, no overlap, no stagger. Give both marks the same visual height rather than the same pixel height — a wide lockup and a compact one at identical heights will not read as equal.

Semantic tokens

Build against these, not the raw palette. They are the layer that changes when the brand or theme changes.

Fill colours and text colours are different tokens --rds-color-primary is the exact brand colour, for fills and large graphics. --rds-color-primary-text is the same colour shifted until it is legible as body text, and --rds-color-primary-strong is the fill that can carry a label at AA. Robo University’s lime is 2.1:1 on white — unusable as text, perfect as a fill. The same pairing exists for accent, success, warning, danger and info.

Roboto Light Caption and annotation

Typesetting and hierarchy

Authored in points, because the guidelines specify print. Screen sizes are pt × 96/72, rounded to the nearest pixel — Body at 12pt lands exactly on 16px and Caption at 9pt on 12px, so the print and screen scales agree rather than merely coexisting. Each role carries its weight, so --rds-text-heading-weight travels with --rds-text-heading.

StyleSpecimenPrintScreenWeightToken
Cover and hero titles Title 60 pt 80 px light --rds-text-title
Page and major section headings Heading 32 pt 43 px bold --rds-text-heading
Standfirst beneath a title Subtitle 24 pt 32 px regular --rds-text-subtitle
Section headers within a page Section Header 16 pt 21 px bold --rds-text-section-header
Inline subheading above a paragraph Subheading 12 pt 16 px bold --rds-text-subheading
Paragraph text Body 12 pt 16 px regular --rds-text-body
Pull quotes Updating humanity 15 pt 20 px regular italic --rds-text-quote
Labels, footnotes Caption 9 pt 12 px light --rds-text-caption
Lead paragraphs extends Body Lg — 18 px regular --rds-text-body-lg
Dense UI text, table cells extends Body Sm — 14 px regular --rds-text-body-sm
Minimum legible size extends Caption Sm — 10 px light --rds-text-caption-sm

Aliases --rds-text-display, -h1, -h2, -h3 and -h4 point at Title, Heading, Subtitle, Section Header and Subheading respectively. They exist so existing markup keeps working; they are not separate sizes.

Japanese — Noto Sans JP

Japanese uses Noto Sans JP across the same four weight roles and the same size scale, so a bilingual page keeps one vertical rhythm. Only the leading changes: Japanese sets without word spaces and with full-height glyphs, so Latin line-heights read as cramped.

How the switch fires The face changes on a real language declaration — <html lang="ja"> for a Japanese page, or <p lang="ja"> for a passage inside an English one. That also tells screen readers to switch voice, which a CSS class cannot do. Where you genuinely cannot set the attribute, .rds-ja applies the same styling. Never mix the two faces inside one sentence.

Loading the fonts

The stylesheet declares the faces but does not fetch them, because that is a hosting decision. Link dist/css/fonts.css before the main stylesheet, or self-host — preferred for production, and worth it for Japanese, where the full family runs to several megabytes if you do not subset it. 

<link rel="stylesheet" href="dist/css/fonts.css">

Using it

1. Load the stylesheet

<link rel="stylesheet" href="dist/css/robo-design-system.css">

2. Declare the brand

<html data-brand="robo-lab">                    <!-- follows the OS for dark -->
<html data-brand="coop-lab" data-theme="dark">   <!-- forced -->
<html data-brand="robo-university" data-theme="light">

Valid brands: robo-coop, robo-lab, robo-university, coop-lab. With no data-brand, the parent brand applies.

3. Build with tokens

.my-panel {
  background: var(--rds-color-surface-raised);
  color:      var(--rds-color-text);
  border:     var(--rds-border-hairline) solid var(--rds-color-border);
  border-radius: var(--rds-radius-lg);
  padding:    var(--rds-space-6);
  box-shadow: var(--rds-elevation-2);
}
